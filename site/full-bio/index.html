The full story

My name is Ben Myburgh. I am a husband, father, Christ follower, pilot, engineer, manager, entrepreneur, business owner and adventurer. The difficult part has always been deciding where the story begins.

Early years

For as long as I can remember, my family owned a business. My father started Pretoria Transit Interiors in the early 1990s, and it was there that I learned to enjoy business and engineering. As his company grew, so did my knowledge of CAD—beginning with AutoCAD in the MS-DOS era. During high school I welded farm equipment for money between CAD projects for my father.

I earned my private pilot certificate during my junior year. After high school, I attended college for two semesters before moving to ATP Flight School to earn my CFI, CFII and MEI ratings.

Murfreesboro Aviation

In 2009, I started a flight school with one aircraft and one instructor. Over three years, Murfreesboro Aviation grew to seven aircraft and a full team. The airplanes flew roughly 100 hours each month, and I accumulated approximately 3,000 hours across everything from floatplanes to business jets.

I sold the company in 2011, shortly after my father's death. I had never finished my engineering degree, and I wanted to pursue the work he had inspired.

My father and the road

In 2009, my father suffered a catastrophic work accident. He spent a week in a coma, endured 17 surgeries and lost his right leg. During his recovery he was also diagnosed with melanoma. He fought through reconstructive surgery and cancer treatment for more than a year before passing away in October 2010.

After losing him and selling the flight school, I left on a motorcycle journey around the world. I rode mountain passes in the Pyrenees and Alps, camped under the stars in Mongolia, crossed some of Russia's most dangerous roads, crashed more than once and repaired the bike with limited supplies. Roughly 25,000 km later, I returned to Tennessee with a different view of the world—and eventually a book.

Engineering

After returning home, I finished my mechanical engineering degree at Middle Tennessee State University. Along the way I worked on SAE Baja and Formula projects, repaired motorcycles, supported electric-aircraft flight testing at Yates ElectroSpace and worked across prototyping, assembly and quality assurance at a manufacturing startup.

During my final semester I joined Nissan's Smyrna plant as a quality engineer responsible for the all-electric Leaf. I later led warranty-reduction projects and a digital innovation initiative involving smart cameras, additive manufacturing and emerging shop-floor technology.

Scaled Composites and Opener

At Scaled Composites, I supported the design and construction of the Stratolaunch center wing as an engineering technician and composites fabricator. The work included drill templates, structural calculations, composite layups and hands-on aircraft assembly.

From 2018 to 2020, I served as Mechanical Design Engineering Manager at Opener, leading structural design work for the BlackFly eVTOL aircraft. I managed multidisciplinary engineering teams and designed aircraft parts and manufacturing tooling in composites, aluminum and wood.

Pretoria Engineering

A few years after my father's death, I rebuilt his engineering business as Pretoria Engineering. The company turns early concepts and “napkin sketches” into practical products through CAD, prototyping, manufacturing and testing.

The work has ranged from consumer products and autonomous-vehicle systems to aircraft modifications, flight-simulation models and Red Bull Air Race development. A hands-on background in machining, fabrication and composites means many prototypes and tools can be designed and built under one roof.

Professional simulation

Simulator work originally grew from projects for the Red Bull Air Race. Sim Your Plane developed into custom hardware and software that can transform real aircraft controls into flight-simulator inputs without permanently modifying the aircraft.

I now work with Laminar Research as the X-Plane 12 Pro support liaison, helping professional simulation customers troubleshoot aircraft models, system behavior and integration problems. That position sits directly between software, engineering and what a pilot expects the aircraft to do.

HMARD

HMARD brings that same physical/digital integration into live flight. It is a helmet-mounted augmented-reality training system designed to place persistent virtual aircraft, targets, boundaries and tactical cues into the real outside world without requiring deep integration into a specific aircraft.

The system combines Unity, stereoscopic displays, aircraft-data processing, custom head tracking and live-flight testing. It has progressed beyond a simulator-only concept and has been tested in maneuvering flight.

Current

Today I split my time between aircraft and mechanical product development, X-Plane professional support, simulator systems, contract flying, flight instruction and airshow operations. My longer-term direction is increasingly centered on experimental aircraft, engineering and flight-test work while turning HMARD into a funded training capability.
